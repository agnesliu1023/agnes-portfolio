Echoes. Pattern. Paths.

Data posters and an NFC-enabled poetry book on people and wildlife along the Providence riverfront.

Overview

Together with Hyunmin Kim and Shiyan Zhu (Ashley), this project explores the relationship between humans and wildlife at Living Edge PVD, an urban riverside space where nature and human activity coexist.

In Phase 1 we created four abstract data posters mapping bird populations, sound levels, human presence and time patterns. Printed on transparent material, the layered display visualizes the interconnected ecological and human dynamics of the site. Phase 2 translates the findings into a visual poetry book that turns sound into visual expression, with NFC for interactive storytelling.

01

Research and gathering

We combined quantitative and qualitative methods. I led data collection — measuring bird population densities, mapping trash locations and observing feeding behaviour at different times of day — and interviewed park visitors about how they interact with wildlife. From this data we designed maps showing the spatial relationships between bird activity, human presence and environmental impact.

02

Findings

Many visitors feed birds bread and other human food — convenient, but unhealthy for the birds over time. Some knew bread was harmful and used it anyway because it was easiest. Human trash also draws birds to ingest harmful materials. Our maps showed the highest bird densities around feeding and trash hotspots, making visible how human activity shapes bird behaviour and health — and the need for education on healthy feeding and responsible waste.

03

Phase 1 — Posters

Four abstract posters — bird populations, sound levels, human presence and time patterns — printed on transparent material and layered so the site’s dynamics read together.

04

Phase 2 — Visual poetry book

We extended the research into a visual poetry book that blends editorial and graphic design to depict the site’s human–wildlife interactions. The French-fold book carries typography and abstract illustrations on the outer pages and original poetry, written during our observations, on the inner folds.

NFC tags let readers tap a phone to open sound and video recorded at the site, extending the poetic and visual storytelling beyond the page.
