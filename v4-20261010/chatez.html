Overview

An AI relationship advisor

ChatEZ reads a dating conversation and helps you decide what to say next. In two months I redesigned it end to end — UX, UI system and brand — and took it from first build to launch.

01

Background

ChatEZ helps college students and working adults read dating conversations and decide how to respond — with conversation analysis, relationship insights and suggested replies.

A first build already worked, but every new feature made it harder to use. I was brought in to rethink the core experience and give the launch one visual identity — not to reskin it.

02

The features were there. Getting started wasn’t.

Two problems stood between users and the help they came for.

01Unclear entry point

Several features competed for attention, and weak hierarchy made it hard to know where to start.

02Disconnected AI workflow

Analysis, reply generation, editing and history did not feel like one continuous task.

How might we

How might we help users move from an emotionally charged conversation to useful, trustworthy guidance and an editable reply — without overwhelming them or taking away their agency?

Approach

Reframe it as one journey

Instead of treating each AI feature as its own destination, I rebuilt the product around one decision: understand the conversation, read the signals, decide what to say.

04

Turning the audit into priorities

I mapped the existing journey and reviewed key screens to find unclear entry points, disconnected interactions and missing context.

01Unclear starting point

Weak hierarchy, little guidance and an inconsistent visual language made it unclear how to begin.

02Limited support for different needs

Users needed more than screenshot-based replies — we had to decide which tasks to support next.

03An incomplete supporting experience

Supporting pages and navigation were underdeveloped, leaving gaps beyond the main reply flow.

05

Three design decisions

Low-fidelity wireframes explored navigation, chat history and Chat Profiles before any visual design. Three decisions came out of them.

Decision 01

Clarify sign-in and entry points

The original sign-in used low-contrast text and hid other login options, and the wider app lacked consistent colour, type and components. I made every sign-in choice visible and set up one reusable UI system.

01Clearer sign-in choices

WeChat, phone and Apple sign-in sit on one screen, with guest access grouped below.

02Consistent hierarchy

Higher text contrast and one button style separate actions from supporting content.

Decision 02

Design around three distinct needs

Interviews with students and working adults surfaced three needs: finding a reply when stuck, refining a draft, and making sense of a longer exchange. Each became its own mode — Generate a Reply, Refine My Reply, Analyze a Conversation — with a clear starting point.

01Clearer starting points

Three modes and clearer upload guidance tell users where to begin and what to expect.

02Insights first

The uploaded screenshot steps back; AI insights and reply suggestions lead.

03Easier-to-scan analysis

Long analyses are split into sections with stronger hierarchy, so the takeaway is easy to find.

04More control over replies

Editing, regeneration and a preview let users adjust a suggestion before copying it.

Decision 03

Keep conversation context connected

Folders and saved chats existed, but saving a conversation, choosing where it goes and revisiting its analysis felt disconnected. I rebuilt this around Chat Profiles, with past analyses — and the option to add more chats — on the profile page.

01Unclear folders

The layout and “Add” label left users unsure what would be saved and where.

02Hidden unfiled chats

Chats outside folders were hard to find, and visible delete icons invited mistakes.

03Thin previews

Image-only thumbnails gave little context about a conversation or its analysis.

04History out of reach

Users had to leave the current screen to reach History.

01Direct access to history

The side menu brings History and Chat Profiles together.

02Clear profile creation

Users create a profile straight from a conversation, with a short explanation of why.

03Informative records

Message previews, timestamps and personality tags give each saved chat context.

04Guided empty states

New profiles explain their purpose and offer one clear “Upload Chats” action.

06

Brand refresh

I refreshed ChatEZ’s identity so the assistant feels approachable and consistent: the logo, the mascot with its expressions and stickers, and one type, spacing and icon language — extended into social campaigns and posters.

Design system

A UI system the team can build with

Heading and body styles, a 4px baseline grid, spacing tokens, a 20px content margin, and corner and stroke rules — every new screen is assembled from the same parts. The full artboard scrolls below.

Launch

Launch and social campaign

The identity carried into the App Store listing, launch posters and social collages.

07

Agent operations dashboard

An internal workspace for managing chat samples, testing prompts and reviewing agent runs: an asset library, a visual workflow and run history in one place. The team inspects each node’s inputs, prompts and outputs, adjusts expert and evaluator prompts, and reopens earlier runs.

01Visual agent workflow

A connected canvas shows how chat inputs move through expert and evaluator prompts to a reply.

02Prompt inspection and testing

Node details expose inputs, prompts, outputs and metadata for testing prompt changes.

03Samples and run history

A shared sample library and run history let the team reuse conversations and reopen runs.

08

Developer handoff and visual QA

I prepared final screens, reusable components and interaction notes for development, then reviewed the build against the designs and documented fixes to spacing, type, icons and component states.

09

Launched in two months

In two months ChatEZ went from a hard-to-use first build to a launched product: one connected flow for analysis, replies and editing, three modes with clear starting points, and Chat Profiles that keep context. The launched product was also part of the startup’s fundraising.

01Outcome

One connected flow, three clear modes, Chat Profiles that keep context — shipped through handoff and visual QA.

02Reflection

Design around the decisions people make with AI: understand the analysis, adjust the suggestion, choose what to send.

03Next step

Test whether users pick the right mode and save a Chat Profile without help — and where they edit replies most.
