01

A city on a truck

Every autumn, cities and brands send floats to the Shanghai Tourism Festival parade, which winds through the city’s districts at night. Each float is a moving advertisement: it has to tell a place’s story at a glance, read from both sides of the street and from far away, glow after dark, and carry four to eight performers with its own generators and sound system — all on a truck chassis.

At Shanghai Arts & Crafts Factory I worked across the factory’s floats for the 2023 festival, and co-designed the float for Nanjing’s Tangshan Hot Spring resort.

02

Drawings the factory could build from

A rendering sells the idea; a factory needs dimensions. I translated concepts into 3D views and three-view drawings fitted to the real truck — cab, wheelbase, overhangs and clearances — so the team could check proportions and sightlines before any steel was cut.

Each float then got a production board — rendering, side elevation, plan and build schedule — reviewed with the factory’s design and production leads.

03

Testing in light

The parade happens after dark, so color was judged with the lights on. The phoenix feathers were cut from acrylic and printed film and laid on a lightbox; colored films and acrylic chips were matched backlit, and printed wraps were proofed at scale before production.

04

Building at full scale

In the factory hall the drawings became steel: decks welded onto truck chassis, sculptural forms bent from steel wire, and lantern elements wrapped and painted by hand. Plywood templates checked feather sizes against a wire wing before it was covered.

I followed the build on site, checking forms against the drawings and revising details when proportions, structure or sightlines changed at full scale.

05

On the street

After a two-year break, the floats were back: from 17 to 27 September, 16 of them toured ten of Shanghai’s districts at night, with the factory’s quality team following every route. On the Shigatse float, snow-mountain lines, Everest and Gesang flowers ran from front to back as one flowing form — linking the Huangpu River to the Yarlung Tsangpo.
